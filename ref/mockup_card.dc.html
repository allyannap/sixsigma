For planners
A zone can meet the 8-minute goal and still be slower than the rest of the city month after month. That gap matters when deciding where the next ambulance or station goes.

Analysis of means (ANOM) on ln(minutes), within one urgency group so zones with more severe calls are not penalized. Center line = overall mean of ln(minutes), shown as {{anG}} min. Decision limits for zone i: center ± h · σ̂ · √((N − nᵢ) / (N · nᵢ)), with pooled within-zone σ̂ = {{anSp}}, N = {{anN}} calls and h = 2.73 (k = 8, α = 0.05, Bonferroni approximation to the ANOM critical value). Whiskers are 95% confidence intervals for each zone's mean.
One-way ANOVA on the same data asks whether any zone differs: F({{anDf1}}, {{anDf2}}) = {{anF}}. ANOM then shows which ones, and in which direction. In the product, the same chart compares counties in the state view and ZIP codes inside a county.

Zone 4: where did the extra minutes come from?

The extra time is on the road, because the trips got longer. Since Oct 3, Google Maps expects Zone 4 drives to take {{gUp}} longer, and ambulances still beat Google's estimate. The 911 center and crews are as quick as before. This points to where ambulances start from (coverage), not to the people on the call.

Fault tree (series system)
Late arrival (total > 8:00) OR ─ Dispatch delay (911 center) ─ Turnout delay (crew) ─ Drive delay OR ─ Long trip (Google ETA above zone normal) ─ Drive slower than Google (actual − ETA)
Each late call is assigned to the basic event with the largest excess over its zone's pre-Oct 3 median. Counts / {{z4late}} give the branch shares.
Extra data this needs, per call
eta_google_s: Google Maps drive time with traffic, from the ambulance's position when assigned to the incident address. Requested at assignment time (Routes / Distance Matrix API, traffic-aware). Traffic at that moment cannot be looked up later, so it must be logged live.
In the synthetic datasets this column is simulated, so the repo runs without a Google API key. Only a live deployment needs one.
